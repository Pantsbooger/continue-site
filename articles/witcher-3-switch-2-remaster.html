Good news: you're not late to The Witcher 3, you're right on time

An eleven year old game just landed a native Switch 2 remaster, which is as good an excuse as any to stop feeling guilty about your backlog.

The Continue? Desk · 12 September 2026 · 5 min read

Image: CD PROJEKT RED

The Witcher 3 came out in 2015. If you're only getting round to it now, somewhere between the fourth toddler bedtime story and the point where you finally remembered to renew the car insurance, you are, by any sensible measure, not late. You're simply playing it at the correct time for your life, which happens to be eleven years after everyone else.

This week's news gives that argument a bit of extra weight. CD Projekt Red's sprawling fantasy epic is getting a native remaster for Switch 2, part of a genuinely stacked September for the console that also includes the Ocarina of Time remake, a new Fire Emblem and Capcom's Onimusha. Early details point to sharper resolution and steadier frame rates in both handheld and docked play, along with loading times that no longer give you time to make a cup of tea, though full patch notes weren't available at the time of writing.

Why "old" is doing a lot of unfair work in that sentence

Games don't have a use-by date. Nobody looks at a well-thumbed novel and complains that it's a decade old, yet gaming culture has a habit of treating anything more than a year past release as a bit of an embarrassment, something you'd rather not admit you're still playing. That's a strange standard to hold a hundred-hour open world epic to, especially one this dense with side quests, decisions and Gwent matches you will absolutely lose to a man in a pub.

There's a genuine case that playing The Witcher 3 now is better than playing it in 2015.

The patches are done, the expansions are cheap, the wiki has already argued out every plot hole, and you get to go in without the noise of launch week telling you what to think about it. You're free to just play the thing.

A short list of other games it's fine to be late to

  • Breath of the Wild still holds up, and if you've somehow avoided every spoiler for nine years that's a genuine achievement worth protecting.
  • Persona 5 Royal rewards the sort of unhurried, one-chapter-a-night pace a lot of us are stuck with anyway.
  • Hades is built for short bursts, so the "I only have twenty minutes" excuse doesn't apply here. It's basically designed for you.
  • Stardew Valley has been quietly running for nearly a decade and will still be there whenever you turn up.

The actual point

Nobody hands out medals for finishing games on release day. The backlog isn't a queue you're falling behind in, it's just a shelf of things that will still be good whenever you get to them. The Witcher 3 waited eleven years for a Switch 2 remaster. It can wait a little longer for you too.

Good news: you're not late to The Witcher 3, whatever you're playing it on

A free remaster is landing on every platform later this month, with a new expansion due in 2027. One console's owners have a particularly good reason to be pleased about it.

Ant · 12 September 2026 · 6 min read

Image: CD PROJEKT RED

The Witcher 3 came out in 2015. If you're only getting round to it now, somewhere between the fourth toddler bedtime story and the point where you finally remembered to renew the car insurance, you are, by any sensible measure, not late. You're simply playing it at the correct time for your life, which happens to be eleven years after everyone else.

The news that prompted this bit of reassurance is a genuinely big one. CD Projekt Red is remastering its sprawling fantasy epic for absolutely everyone, not one platform's worth of players. The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt Remastered arrives on 29 September on PC, PS5, Xbox Series X|S and Switch 2, and it's a free upgrade if you already own the game on a qualifying version of any of those. Everyone gets the Hearts of Stone and Blood and Wine expansions thrown in too, along with reworked combat, movement, monster behaviour, Witcher Sign effects and horse handling that, by most accounts, was overdue about eight years ago.

There's more coming after that. A brand new expansion, Songs of the Past, was announced alongside the remaster and is due in 2027, sending Geralt to Dandelion's homeland for what CD Projekt Red is pitching as a proper full-scale adventure rather than a quick side trip. So whichever box or PC you play on, there's a decent stretch of Witcher left to get through before anyone can accuse you of having finished the series too early.

Switch owners in particular have earned this one

Everyone benefits from a remaster, but nobody has earned it quite like the Switch faithful. When Saber Interactive squeezed the entirety of The Witcher 3, DLC and all, onto a single Switch cartridge back in 2019, it was treated as something close to witchcraft. The team's own boss admitted the early build ran at 10fps and needed 50% more memory than the console actually had. That it worked at all, on a tablet you could play on the sofa, felt like a small miracle at the time.

That miracle has aged, though. Since Switch 2 launched, Witcher 3 has mostly just been the same 2019 version running in backwards compatibility, which is fine, but a long way from what the game looks like on anything released this decade. A full native remaster puts Switch 2 owners on genuinely level footing with everyone else for the first time, rather than getting the polite, slightly blurry cousin of the version PC and PlayStation players have been enjoying.

Why "old" is doing a lot of unfair work in that sentence

Games don't have a use-by date. Nobody looks at a well-thumbed novel and complains that it's a decade old, yet gaming culture has a habit of treating anything more than a year past release as a bit of an embarrassment, something you'd rather not admit you're still playing. That's a strange standard to hold a hundred-hour open world epic to, especially one this dense with side quests, decisions and Gwent matches you will absolutely lose to a man in a pub.

There's a genuine case that playing The Witcher 3 now is better than playing it in 2015.

The patches are done, the expansions are cheap, the wiki has already argued out every plot hole, and you get to go in without the noise of launch week telling you what to think about it. You're free to just play the thing.

A short list of other games it's fine to be late to

  • Breath of the Wild still holds up, and if you've somehow avoided every spoiler for nine years that's a genuine achievement worth protecting.
  • Persona 5 Royal rewards the sort of unhurried, one-chapter-a-night pace a lot of us are stuck with anyway.
  • Hades is built for short bursts, so the "I only have twenty minutes" excuse doesn't apply here. It's basically designed for you.
  • Stardew Valley has been quietly running for nearly a decade and will still be there whenever you turn up.

One thing to enjoy while it lasts

A small note of caution for Switch 2 owners getting comfortable. Songs of the Past has been confirmed for PC, PS5 and Xbox Series X|S. Switch 2 doesn't appear on that list, at least not yet, which may just be a scheduling detail or may not be.

Whatever happens with the expansion, the bigger reckoning is The Witcher 4. CD Projekt Red's own finance team has already ruled out a 2026 launch, with most estimates now landing somewhere around 2027 or 2028. Everything about it points to a proper next-generation production built around PS5 and Xbox Series hardware, the sort of game that doesn't tend to turn up on a console that also plays handheld indie platformers on the school run. Switch 2 owners would be sensible to treat this remaster, and whatever of the new expansion eventually arrives, as a very good last hurrah for Geralt on their console for the foreseeable future.

The actual point

Nobody hands out medals for finishing games on release day. The backlog isn't a queue you're falling behind in, it's just a shelf of things that will still be good whenever you get to them. The Witcher 3 waited eleven years for a remaster that finally treats every platform the same way. Wherever you play it, it can wait a little longer for you too, though if you're on Switch 2 it might be worth not leaving it quite as long as last time.

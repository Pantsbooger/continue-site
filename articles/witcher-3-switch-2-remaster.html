Good news: you're not late to The Witcher 3, whatever you're playing it on

A free remaster lands on every platform on 29 September, with a new expansion already confirmed for 2027. If you've never played it, there might not be a better moment to start.

Clarkey · 12 September 2026 · 6 min read

Image: CD PROJEKT RED

The Witcher 3 came out in 2015. If you're only getting round to it now, somewhere between the fourth toddler bedtime story and the point where you finally remembered to renew the car insurance, you are, by any sensible measure, not late. You're simply playing it at the correct time for your life, which happens to be eleven years after everyone else.

The news that prompted this bit of reassurance is a genuinely big one. CD Projekt Red is remastering its sprawling fantasy epic for absolutely everyone, not one platform's worth of players. The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt Remastered arrives on 29 September on PC, PS5, Xbox Series X|S and Switch 2, and it's a free upgrade if you already own the game on a qualifying version of any of those. Everyone gets the Hearts of Stone and Blood and Wine expansions thrown in too, along with reworked combat, movement, monster behaviour, Witcher Sign effects and horse handling that, by most accounts, was overdue about eight years ago.

There's more coming after that. A brand new expansion, Songs of the Past, was announced alongside the remaster and is due in 2027, sending Geralt to Dandelion's homeland for what CD Projekt Red is pitching as a proper full-scale adventure rather than a quick side trip. That's the bit worth sitting with: this isn't a museum piece getting dusted off one last time, it's a game still actively growing, with a publisher clearly convinced there's an audience left to grow it for.

Why now is actually the smart time to start

None of this reads like an eleven year old game any more. Reworked combat and movement, smarter monsters and a new expansion on the way put The Witcher 3 in the odd position of getting more relevant with age rather than less, which is not a sentence you can say about most 2015 releases. If you start it now, you're not settling for the version everyone else already played and moved on from, you're getting the best the game has ever looked or played, right as it happens rather than years after the fact.

There's a real argument for timing your first playthrough to land around 29 September specifically. Geralt's story is long enough that you won't finish it in a weekend anyway, so starting now, or picking it back up if you stalled out years ago, means you'll hit the improved combat and monster encounters with fresh eyes just as the remaster properly lands, rather than adapting to a patched-in update halfway through a save you started on the old version.

Why "old" is doing a lot of unfair work in that sentence

Games don't have a use-by date. Nobody looks at a well-thumbed novel and complains that it's a decade old, yet gaming culture has a habit of treating anything more than a year past release as a bit of an embarrassment, something you'd rather not admit you're still playing. That's a strange standard to hold a hundred-hour open world epic to, especially one this dense with side quests, decisions and Gwent matches you will absolutely lose to a man in a pub.

There's a genuine case that playing The Witcher 3 now is better than playing it in 2015.

The patches are done, the expansions are cheap, the wiki has already argued out every plot hole, and you get to go in without the noise of launch week telling you what to think about it. You're free to just play the thing.

A short list of other games it's fine to be late to

  • Breath of the Wild still holds up, and if you've somehow avoided every spoiler for nine years that's a genuine achievement worth protecting.
  • Persona 5 Royal rewards the sort of unhurried, one-chapter-a-night pace a lot of us are stuck with anyway.
  • Hades is built for short bursts, so the "I only have twenty minutes" excuse doesn't apply here. It's basically designed for you.
  • Stardew Valley has been quietly running for nearly a decade and will still be there whenever you turn up.

One of the best RPGs going, not just one of the oldest

Strip away the release date and judge The Witcher 3 purely on what it does: a genuinely enormous world where the side quests are frequently better written than other games' main stories, combat and crafting that are only getting sharper with this remaster, and a lead character with more personality than most franchises manage across an entire trilogy. With Songs of the Past confirmed for 2027, this isn't a game you're catching up on before it gets retired, it's one that's still being actively added to. That combination, a beloved back catalogue plus new content still on the way, is rare enough to be worth planning around rather than politely ignoring.
